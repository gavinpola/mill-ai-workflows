Four Paths: From Vendor to Infrastructure

Mill starts as a waste tool. The question is whether it stays there or evolves into something with a higher multiple and larger addressable value.

Path Revenue Potential Multiple Valuation Ceiling
A. Waste Tool $60M ARR (10K locations × $500/mo) 5x ~$300M
B. Food Ops Platform $240M ARR (10K locations × $2K/mo) 10x ~$2.4B
C. Industry Data Layer $400M ARR (customer + CPG revenue) 15x ~$6B
D. Bloomberg of Food $600M+ ARR (industry standard) 18x ~$10B+

What Has to Be True for Each Path

Path A (Waste Tool): Hardware works, customers reduce waste, basic retention. This is the default if nothing else changes.

Path B (Platform): Platform expansion works—customers adopt food cost, labor, and procurement modules. NRR exceeds 115%. Requires building those modules and proving adoption.

Path C (Data Layer): CPG data revenue materializes. $50M+ from data sales to Kraft, Unilever, Tyson. Requires 500+ kitchens for statistical significance and CPG sales capability.

Path D (Bloomberg): Mill's data becomes essential for the industry. Every CPG, retailer, and chain needs access. Network effects make the dataset unreplicable. This requires becoming an industry standard—not just a successful vendor.

The Strategic Question

Path A is where Mill starts. The decisions made in 2025-2027 determine whether Path B, C, or D is possible. Product roadmap, pricing structure, and GTM strategy all influence which ceiling Mill hits.

Revenue Ceilings: What Caps Growth

These ceilings aren't predictions—they're constraints to plan around. Each requires specific capabilities to break through.

Ceiling 1: Waste-Only Revenue ~$60M ARR max
Why it exists: Waste is estimated at 4-10% of food costs (industry sources vary). At $500/month, 10K locations = $60M ARR ceiling for waste-only value.
How to break through: Expand from waste tracking → food cost management. The hypothesis: waste classification data enables menu-level cost analysis (linking waste to specific items, portions, prep methods). This requires building that analytical layer—not automatic from waste data alone.
Ceiling 2: Per-Location Revenue ~$3K/month max
Why it exists: Even as a full food operations platform, there's a ceiling on what any single location will pay for software.
How to break through: Enterprise deals (one customer, 10,000 locations). International expansion. Adjacent markets (grocery, manufacturing, distribution).
Ceiling 3: Customer-Paid Revenue ~$400M ARR max
Why it exists: Even with maximum penetration and maximum per-location revenue, customer-paid revenue has limits. $400M is illustrative, not a hard ceiling.
How to break through: CPG data revenue (hypothesis: they'd pay for item-level waste intelligence— needs validation with actual CPG conversations). Other potential streams (financial services, regulatory) are speculative until market demand is tested.
Ceiling 4: US Market ~50K locations at 5%
Why it exists: US commercial kitchens are ~1M locations. Even with strong penetration, the addressable market has limits.
How to break through: International expansion (EU, UK, APAC). Adjacent verticals (hotels, hospitals, schools, corporate cafeterias). Upstream expansion (food manufacturing).

What Has to Be Proven (and When)

Each stage has specific proof points that unlock the next. Missing these creates risk; hitting them early creates optionality.

Pre-Launch Now → 2027
  • CV accuracy >90% on common food items (demonstrable in test environment)
  • Hardware reliability validated (MTBF, installation cost, maintenance model)
  • Unit economics work (hardware cost vs. base fee—does the math close?)
  • 3-5 design partners signed (LOIs or pilot agreements with recognizable brands)
Year 1 2027 → 2028
  • Waste reduction claims hold (15-25% documented in customer environments)
  • Customers don't churn after learning (retention >80% annual)
  • Revenue per location matches model ($500-800/month realized)
  • CAC/LTV sustainable (payback <18 months)
  • 50+ paying locations with referenceable case studies
Year 2-3 2028 → 2030
  • Platform expansion works (customers adopt food cost module, NRR >115%)
  • Enterprise motion works (3+ accounts with >50 locations each)
  • CPG data has buyers (first $500K+ contract signed)
  • 500+ locations with food cost module live and generating revenue
Year 4-5 2030 → 2032
  • Network effects are real (benchmark accuracy improves with more kitchens; customers stay for peer comparison data they can't get elsewhere)
  • International works (EU or UK market with 50+ locations)
  • Multiple revenue streams (customer + CPG + advisory all material)
  • IPO-ready metrics ($100M+ ARR, NRR >110%, gross margin >70%)

Soft Requirements

The multiple question: How markets perceive Mill will determine the multiple. The positioning story matters, but only if backed by metrics that support it (NRR, data revenue, competitive moat). Claiming "data layer" while showing "waste tool" metrics won't work.

The Meta-Decision

Are we building a waste tool that might become a platform, or a platform that starts with waste? The answer shapes product, pricing, and GTM. If the answer is "waste tool," the ceiling is $300M and we should optimize for profitability. If the answer is "platform," we need to invest in capabilities that don't pay off until Year 3-4.

What Could Break the Thesis

Honest assessment of risks. These aren't reasons not to proceed; they're things to watch and mitigate.

Risk Impact Mitigation
Hardware economics don't work Base fee can't cover costs; margin collapse Validate unit costs before scaling production
Learn-and-leave proves real LTV collapses; CAC doesn't pay back Build compounding predictions; deep integrations
CPG data doesn't sell Ceiling is Path B, not C/D Early CPG conversations to validate demand
Competition commoditizes Multiple compresses; price pressure Build moats early; first-mover on data network
Platform expansion doesn't stick NRR stays flat; ceiling is lower Build modules that solve real problems, not features

A key hypothesis: The value is in data, not hardware. Hardware is the collection mechanism. This hypothesis is unproven—it assumes the data is differentiated and valuable. If CV accuracy plateaus or the insights aren't actionable, the "data company" thesis collapses.

Three Valuations, Three Requirements

$3B: Requires ~$250M ARR with platform-level retention (NRR >110%). Becomes viable if Year 1-3 proof points hold and platform expansion works.

$5B: Requires ~$300M+ ARR plus meaningful data revenue. Becomes viable if CPG data demand materializes and NRR exceeds 115%. Higher uncertainty.

$10B: Requires Mill to become industry infrastructure—not just a successful vendor. This is the highest-risk, highest-reward path. Most companies that aim for this don't reach it.

What to Do Now

  • Product: Build for platform, not just waste. Food cost module by Year 2.
  • Pricing: Expansion terms in initial contract. Data rights preserved.
  • GTM: Land with waste, expand with platform. Enterprise motion by Year 3.
  • Data: Early CPG conversations to validate demand. Build aggregation infrastructure.

The path to $10B isn't guaranteed—it requires becoming an industry standard. But the path to $3-5B is achievable with strong execution. The decisions made in 2025-2027 determine which ceiling Mill hits.
