Pricing Strategy: The Complete Picture

Not "here's our pricing" but "here's how we evaluated alternatives and landed on a model that works for us AND the customer while preserving lifetime value." Seven models analyzed. One recommended.

The Multi-Year Problem

Every pricing conversation starts here: How do you charge for value that the customer eventually "learns"?

The Learn-and-Churn Timeline

Year 1: Customer doesn't know what's being wasted. Mill reveals: chicken waste spikes Fridays, over-prep on Mondays. Customer acts, reduces waste 20%. Mill takes 15% of savings. Good revenue.

Year 2: Customer already optimized the obvious stuff. Incremental improvement is maybe 5%. Mill's outcome share drops significantly. Customer thinks: "I already learned this. Why am I paying?"

Year 3: Customer is fully optimized. Marginal gains are tiny. Outcome-based revenue approaches zero. Customer churns.

This is the core problem. Pure outcome-based pricing on "waste reduction learning" creates a self-defeating revenue model. The better you perform in Year 1, the less you can charge in Year 2+.

What Actually Creates Year 2+ Value

Be brutally honest about what does and doesn't work:

Creates Ongoing Value Why It Doesn't Diminish
Trash savings Physical dehydration continues regardless of what customer "learned"
Staff turnover training ~75% annual turnover in restaurants (National Restaurant Association data). New employees make old mistakes. Continuous data = continuous training.
Menu changes New items create new waste patterns. Requires new insights.
Seasonal patterns Only emerge with 12+ months of data. Year 1 data makes Year 2 predictions better.
Compliance documentation Ongoing regulatory requirement. Can't replicate with "learned insights."
Benchmark access "How do I compare to peers?" requires ongoing access to Mill's network data.
Does NOT Create Ongoing Value Why It's Extractable
"We learned your waste patterns" Once learned, it's learned. Knowledge doesn't require subscription.
"We'll send you reports" Reports aren't valuable if insights are stale.
"Continuous monitoring" If a $15/hour employee can weigh trash and get same result, this isn't defensible.

The Key Reframe

The problem isn't that outcome-based pricing doesn't work. The problem is framing outcome as "one-time waste reduction learning."

Better frame: Outcome = "continuous optimization in a changing environment"

What changes: Staff (~75% annual turnover per NRA data), menus (seasonal, new items), volume (holidays, events, weather), costs (ingredient prices shift), regulations (new compliance requirements).

Static optimization doesn't work in dynamic environments. The customer who "learned" their waste patterns in January doesn't know their summer patterns, holiday patterns, or new menu item patterns.

Pricing Models: Deep Analysis

Seven models, evaluated against Mill's specific context. Each with honest pros, cons, and a verdict.

Model 1: Pure Subscription Use as foundation
$500-800/month flat fee, no outcome component

Customer pays fixed amount regardless of results. Simple to explain, simple to budget. Mill captures value regardless of customer behavior.

Why it works
  • Predictable revenue for Mill
  • Trash savings justify base fee alone
  • Works for compliance customers
Why it's not enough
  • No proof of value to customer
  • Customer questions ROI after Year 1
  • No incentive to keep improving

Verdict: Use as foundation (base fee), but not sufficient alone for optimization value. Compliance-driven customers may accept this structure.

Model 2: Pure Outcome-Based Not viable
10-20% of documented savings, no base fee

Customer pays only for proven results. Zero risk to customer. Mill only earns when value is delivered.

Why it's appealing
  • Makes "yes" extremely easy
  • Aligns incentives perfectly
  • Forces Mill to prove value
Why it fails
  • Year 1 big, Year 2+ diminishes
  • Attribution fights ("Mill or new chef?")
  • Revenue unpredictable
  • Doesn't capture trash savings

Why Sierra can do this but Mill can't: AI agent resolution is unambiguous (ticket solved or not). Waste reduction attribution is messy (multiple factors affect food costs). Sierra's "outcome" is instantaneous; Mill's is cumulative over months.

Verdict: Not viable as primary model. Too much attribution complexity, revenue instability.

Model 3: Hybrid (Base + Outcome Share) Best foundation
$300-500/month base + 10-15% of documented savings

Base fee covers hardware and ongoing trash savings. Outcome share kicks in when food cost optimization delivers results. Customer gets "insurance" plus "upside."

Why it works
  • Base fee ensures revenue floor
  • Outcome share proves value
  • Base defensible via trash savings
  • Outcome share is upside, not core
Challenges to solve
  • Still need attribution methodology
  • Year 2+ outcome share will decline
  • Two components to explain
The Year 2+ problem in hybrid
Year 1: $400 base + $300 outcome = $700/month
Year 2: $400 base + $100 outcome = $500/month (basics already optimized)
Year 3: $400 base + $50 outcome = $450/month
Revenue declining. Need mechanism to address.

Verdict: Best foundation. Use as starting point and add mechanisms to address Year 2+ value (see Model 5).

Model 4: Declining Outcome Share Over Time Strategically wrong
Year 1: 20%, Year 2: 15%, Year 3: 10%, Year 4+: 5%

Explicitly recognizes that early wins are biggest. Customer sees price "decreasing" over time. Mill front-loads revenue.

Why it seems appealing
  • Matches reality (big wins early)
  • Customer likes "cheaper over time"
  • Predictable decline vs. unpredictable
Why it's wrong
  • Incentivizes churn ("I'll leave eventually")
  • Devalues long-term relationship
  • Opposite of what we want
  • Leaves money on table

Verdict: Reject. This is the opposite of the strategy we need. We want INCREASING value over time, not decreasing.

Model 5: Escalating Value + Price (Platform Expansion) Recommended
Base fee + modules that unlock over time

Customer starts with core value. New value unlocks as data accumulates. Each new module is a natural upsell. Price increases with value delivered.

The expansion model
Year 1: $500/month (waste tracking + trash savings)
Year 2: +$200/month (food cost module - unlock after 12 months)
Year 3: +$150/month (procurement insights - unlock after scale)
Year 4: +$100/month (benchmark network - meaningful at scale)
Revenue increases despite outcome share declining
Why it works
  • Solves the decline problem
  • Matches data value curve
  • Natural expansion, not extraction
  • Retention through value
Requirements
  • Must build the modules (can't be vaporware)
  • Must prove each module's ROI
  • Year 1 must be compelling enough to start

Verdict: This is the strategic direction. Requires product investment but solves multi-year problem. Customer isn't "paying more for same thing"—they're getting new things.

Model 6: Enterprise Data Subscription Use for enterprise
Annual commitment + 3-5% escalator + inflation

Customer commits to annual contract with built-in escalator. Positions Mill as "data infrastructure" not "optimization tool."

Enterprise annual trajectory
Year 1: $600/month × 12 = $7,200/year
Year 2: $7,200 × 1.04 = $7,488/year
Year 3: $7,488 × 1.04 = $7,787/year

The framing shift: NOT "We help you save on food costs." YES "We provide continuous access to operational intelligence."

Verdict: Use for enterprise customers. Pairs well with platform expansion model. Procurement teams understand annual contracts with escalators.

Model 7: Guaranteed Savings (Risk Reversal) Use selectively
"We guarantee X% reduction OR you don't pay"

Mill takes on all the risk. Customer has zero downside to try. Confidence signal that Mill believes in the product.

Why it's appealing
  • Makes "yes" trivially easy
  • Cuts through objections
  • Shows confidence
Why it's dangerous
  • Adverse selection risk
  • Customer could game it
  • No base for hardware cost
  • Margin pressure

Safe structure: "We guarantee 15% waste reduction in Year 1 OR we reduce your Year 2 base fee by the shortfall." Risk reversal + tied to renewal.

Verdict: Use selectively for high-value prospects who need confidence signal. Not primary model.

Making ROI Obvious

How do you make the "yes" decision trivially easy? Address the four reasons customers hesitate.

Why Customers Hesitate

  1. Uncertainty: "Will this actually work for MY kitchen?"
  2. Attribution: "How do I know the savings came from Mill?"
  3. Complexity: "What exactly am I paying for?"
  4. Commitment: "What if I want to cancel?"

The Trash Savings Calculator

Before they even sign, show positive ROI on trash savings alone:

Pre-sale ROI calculation
Your current trash costs: $X/month (they know this)
Food waste as % of trash: ~40-50%
Mill reduction in food waste volume: ~80%
Estimated savings: $Y/month on trash alone
Mill fee: $500/month
Net: You save $Z/month BEFORE food cost optimization

The food cost optimization is upside. Trash savings alone justify the fee. This reframes the decision from "gamble on insights" to "guaranteed savings plus upside."

The Pilot-to-Expand Model

"Start with 3 locations. We'll prove results in 90 days. If you see the savings, expand to all locations."

This de-risks the decision:

  • Limited commitment (3 locations, not 300)
  • Proof before scale (see results before expansion)
  • Natural expansion path (expansion terms in initial contract)

The ROI Dashboard

Make value visible continuously, not just at renewal:

  • Day 1: Real-time waste tracking live
  • Week 1: First waste classifications visible
  • Month 1: First savings estimate calculated
  • Month 3: Accumulated savings vs. cost comparison

Customer sees value continuously. No "what am I paying for?" questions at renewal.

The Guarantee Option (For High-Value Prospects)

The safe guarantee structure
"We guarantee a minimum of $X in documented savings in Year 1, or we'll credit the difference against your Year 2 fee."

This is:

  • Not a free trial (there's still a base fee)
  • Risk reversal (customer is protected)
  • Confidence signal (Mill believes in the product)
  • Retention mechanism (credit against Year 2 = customer stays)

Preserving Customer Satisfaction

Price should track value, not extract value. If price goes up 20%, value should go up 25%+. This is the opposite of "lock them in and extract."

What Causes Dissatisfaction

  1. Feeling overcharged: "I'm paying more but not getting more"
  2. Extraction mindset: "They're always trying to upsell me"
  3. Poor support: "When something breaks, no one helps"
  4. Hidden costs: "I didn't know that was extra"
  5. Value fade: "I don't see results anymore"

How to Preserve Satisfaction

1. Price increases come with value increases

NEVER: "Your price is going up 5% because of inflation"
ALWAYS: "You're now eligible for our Food Cost module, which includes X, Y, Z. This adds $200/month."

Customer pays more, but gets more. No resentment.

2. Make expansion opt-in, not forced

"You've been with Mill for 12 months. Your data now enables seasonal predictions. Would you like to add the Food Cost module?"

Customer chooses expansion, feels in control.

3. Visible value dashboard

Every month, customer sees:

  • "Total savings this month: $X"
  • "Waste diverted: X lbs"
  • "Year-to-date savings: $Y"

Customer sees value continuously, not just at renewal.

4. Fair outcome share calculation

Transparent methodology:

  • Baseline established in Month 1
  • Savings calculated against rolling baseline (accounts for natural fluctuation)
  • Customer can see the calculation

No attribution arguments.

The NPS preservation principle: If price goes up 20%, value should go up 25%+. If value stagnates, price should stagnate. This is "grow together," not "lock them in and extract."

Summary

The multi-year problem is real.

Pure outcome-based pricing on "waste reduction learning" creates declining revenue. Year 1 wins diminish by Year 2+. Don't fight it—add new value on top.

Platform expansion solves it.

Outcome share declines, but new modules (food cost, benchmark, procurement) more than offset. Total revenue: $750/month Year 1 → $1,200/month Year 4.

Make ROI obvious with trash savings.

Lead with the calculator: positive ROI on trash savings alone. Food cost optimization is upside. Pilot-to-expand de-risks the decision.

Data flywheel is mechanical, not hand-wavy.

Data flywheel targets: Month 1 ~70% accuracy, Month 12 ~88% (requires validation). Network scale unlocks benchmarks at 500 kitchens, industry intelligence at 5,000.

Price tracks value, doesn't extract it.

If price goes up 20%, value goes up 25%+. Expansion is opt-in. Dashboard shows value continuously. This preserves NPS.

Data goes beyond customer revenue.

CPG partnerships, procurement intelligence, financial services, labor optimization. These are Year 4+ plays that don't cannibalize customer revenue.
