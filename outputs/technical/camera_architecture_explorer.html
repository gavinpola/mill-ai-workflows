Mill Commercial technical architecture

A deep dive into the hardware and software systems that power commercial food waste tracking. Built for enterprise scale, accuracy, and actionable insights.

System overview

How Mill Commercial turns food waste into actionable procurement intelligence.

Hardware layer

Smart bin with sensors

Industrial-grade container with integrated camera, weight sensors, and depth sensing for accurate food identification and volume measurement.

Software layer

AI-powered recognition

Real-time food classification using computer vision, trained on 500+ food categories with continuous learning from corrections.

Analytics layer

Procurement insights

Pattern detection, anomaly alerts, and data-driven recommendations that connect waste data to purchasing decisions.

The key insight

Mill's consumer product uses weight and moisture sensors (no camera needed for home use). Commercial settings require computer vision because you need to identify what was wasted, not just how much. That's the data that drives procurement optimization.

Why edge processing matters

Processing images locally means faster response times (under 200ms), reduced bandwidth costs, and the system works even when internet is spotty. Only aggregated data and flagged items are sent to the cloud.

Model training approach

Transfer learning

Consumer data advantage

Mill's consumer product generates millions of food waste images. This data pre-trains models before commercial deployment.

Location tuning

Site-specific models

Each location develops its own fine-tuned model. A bakery's classifier gets better at bread; a deli's learns meats.

Continuous improvement

Active learning

Every correction teaches

When staff correct a misclassification, that example enters the training queue. Models improve weekly.

Edge case handling

Graceful uncertainty

When the model isn't sure, it says so. "Unknown item - please classify" is better than a wrong guess.

Why custom models matter

Off-the-shelf food recognition works for consumer photos but struggles with commercial waste. Partially eaten items, mixed containers, and unusual angles require training data from actual waste scenarios. Mill's consumer product provides this unique data advantage.

Pattern detection

Find the "waste signature"

Every location has patterns: Sunday prep waste, Friday spoilage, seasonal spikes. The system learns these and flags deviations.

Anomaly alerts

Catch problems early

"Beef waste up 40% vs. last week" triggers an alert. Staff investigate before it becomes a trend.

Procurement connection

Close the loop

If you consistently waste 15% of romaine orders, maybe order 15% less. The data makes the case.

The attribution challenge

Connecting waste reduction to specific actions is hard. Did waste drop because of Mill, or because the menu changed? The solution: capture baseline data for 60-90 days before any intervention, then measure the same metrics post-deployment with adjustments for seasonality and menu changes.

Operational costs

Ongoing expenses

Cloud compute: ~$5/unit/month. Model training: ~$2/unit/month. Support allocation: ~$8/unit/month. Total: ~$15/unit/month.

ROI framing

A single unit tracking $50,000/month in food purchases only needs to identify 3-5% savings to pay for itself in under a year. At 10% savings (achievable with procurement optimization), payback is under 6 months.
