How should we see food waste?

12 engineering approaches for classifying food waste in commercial kitchens. Ranked by accuracy, friction, feasibility, cost, maintenance, and differentiation.

01 - The challenge

Food waste classification is hard

When a kitchen worker dumps a plate of scraps, we need to identify what's being thrown away. This data becomes procurement intelligence. But the physics are challenging.

*

Mixed pile occlusion

Food gets dumped as a pile. Items on top hide items below. A single camera angle misses 30-50% of the waste.

!

Zero friction tolerance

Back-of-house staff can't slow down. Any system that changes their workflow will fail adoption. 10/10 friction score or nothing.

~

Variable conditions

Kitchen lighting changes. Surfaces are wet, greasy, reflective. Items are crushed, mixed, steaming. No controlled environment.

>

85%+ accuracy required

Below 85%, the data isn't actionable. Procurement decisions need confidence. False positives erode trust.

The Kaleidoscope

Mirror Multi-Angle System

Single high-resolution camera combined with 4-6 precision-angled first-surface mirrors captures 4-6 simultaneous viewing angles in a single frame. The Weighmaster

High-Frequency Weight Dynamics

Four precision load cells under the bin corners sample at 100+ Hz, capturing "impact signatures" when items are dropped. Each food type creates a unique weight fingerprint - the curve of impact, oscillation, and settling. This data validates or corrects vision-based classification, achieving sensor fusion without adding visual complexity.

The Photo Booth

Drop-Through Tunnel
4.05

How it works: Vertical transparent tube creates a "freefall zone." Items fall through and are imaged by cameras around the circumference. Gravity naturally sequences items dumped together.

Why it's interesting: Highest accuracy potential of all options (92-97%). Solves occlusion through physics - items physically separate during freefall.

Hardware

Polycarbonate tube (~$80), 3-4 cameras (~$300 ea), LED strips, raised funnel opening

Accuracy

92-97% (highest)

Cost Delta

+30% vs baseline

#3

The Weighmaster

Weight Dynamics
4.05

How it works: High-frequency load cells (100+ Hz sampling) capture impact signatures. Each food type creates a unique "weight fingerprint" that validates or corrects vision classification.

Why it's interesting: Cheapest add-on (+5% cost), zero user friction, and provides orthogonal data channel for sensor fusion. Weight data also enables mass-based pricing.

Hardware

4x precision load cells (~$100 total), HX711 amplifier (~$5), mounting plate (~$50)

Accuracy

55-70% standalone, 90-95% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+5% vs baseline

Risk: Kitchen vibration interference; requires calibration; floor must be level
BASELINE

The Sculptor

Structured Light (Current Plan)
4.00

How it works: IR pattern projection + camera for precise 3D reconstruction. Tracks volume changes between dumps. This is the current baseline being built.

Why it's the baseline: Proven technology (Intel RealSense), known integration path, reliable depth sensing, good balance across all criteria.

Hardware

Basler ace2 8MP ($900), Intel RealSense D455 ($349), NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX ($599), LED ring ($250)

Accuracy

85-92% classification, +/-2-5% volume

Total Cost

~$2,947/unit (baseline reference)

Risk: Wet surface failure with liquids/soups; depth noise in steam
#5

The Fingerprinter

NIR Spectral Imaging
3.95

How it works: Near-infrared imaging captures material composition (water, sugar, protein content). Identifies food by chemistry, not just appearance.

Why it's interesting: Can differentiate foods that look identical (chicken vs pork, different apple varieties). Orthogonal to visual classification.

Hardware

NIR LED array (~$150), NIR camera module (~$400-800), updated ML model

Accuracy

80-92% standalone, 94-98% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+25% vs baseline

Risk: SWIR version extremely expensive ($10K+); NIR limited to surface composition
#6

The Listener

Acoustic Sensing
3.80

How it works: MEMS microphones capture impact sounds. Different foods have different acoustic signatures when hitting the bin - a carrot sounds different from pasta.

Why it's interesting: Cheapest add-on (2% cost increase), zero user friction, and provides temporal sequencing data. Could distinguish items hitting simultaneously.

Hardware

4-8 MEMS microphones (~$5 each = $20-40), audio ADC board (~$30)

Accuracy

50-70% standalone, 90-95% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+2% vs baseline

Risk: Kitchen background noise; requires careful acoustic isolation; limited standalone accuracy
#7

The Heat Map

Thermal Differential
3.75

How it works: LWIR thermal camera detects temperature signatures. Fresh waste is warm; thermal contrast aids segmentation between recently discarded items and older waste.

Why it's interesting: Provides temporal information (fresh vs old waste) and aids segmentation. Works in any lighting condition.

Hardware

LWIR thermal camera (~$300-500), thermal-RGB fusion software

Accuracy

60-75% standalone, 88-94% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+20% vs baseline

Risk: Cold waste from refrigerators breaks thermal assumptions; soup/liquid thermal bleed
#8

The Tumbler

Rotating Sieve
3.55

How it works: Motorized rotating sieve in lid separates items as they fall through. High-speed camera captures individual items during separation.

Why it's interesting: Mechanical separation solves occlusion definitively. Each item imaged individually before reaching the pile.

Hardware

Stainless steel sieve drum (~$200), stepper motor + controller (~$100), high-speed camera (~$500 upgrade)

Accuracy

88-94%

Cost Delta

+40% vs baseline

Risk: Jamming with large items; mechanical failure; different user experience; 7/10 friction
#9

The Scanner

Capacitive/RF Sensing
3.45

How it works: Capacitive antenna array in bin walls measures dielectric properties. Can "see" through the pile without line-of-sight.

Why it's interesting: Only approach that can see buried items without physical manipulation. Theoretically solves occlusion completely.

Hardware

Custom capacitive array (~$200), signal processing board (~$150), RF shielding

Accuracy

40-55% standalone, 85-92% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+15% vs baseline

Risk: Similar water content = similar dielectric signature; limited food differentiation; novel tech
#10

The Penetrator

Radar/Microwave
3.40

How it works: mmWave radar penetrates surface layer, seeing items buried in the pile. Similar to automotive radar but tuned for organic materials.

Why it's interesting: Radar works through steam, grease, and variable lighting. Proven sensor technology from automotive industry.

Hardware

TI IWR1443 mmWave sensor (~$100-200), signal processing integration

Accuracy

50-65% standalone, 88-93% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+20% vs baseline

Risk: Limited spatial resolution; water absorption; novel application domain
#11

The Cascade

Multi-Stage Conveyor
3.30

How it works: Short inclined conveyor belt spreads items over time. Converts instantaneous dump into temporal sequence for individual imaging.

Why it's interesting: Very high accuracy potential (90-95%). Each item physically separated and imaged individually. Similar to industrial sorting systems.

Hardware

Food-grade belt (~$150), motor system (~$200), extended housing

Accuracy

90-95%

Cost Delta

+50% vs baseline

Risk: Belt cleaning critical; bacteria harbor risk; significant form factor change; 8/10 friction
#12

The Polarizer

Polarimetric Imaging
3.25

How it works: Polarizing filter differentiates organic vs synthetic materials. Cuts through glare and reflections that confuse standard cameras.

Why it's interesting: Can distinguish plastic contamination, identify material types by surface properties, reduce reflection-induced classification errors.

Hardware

Polarizing filter array (~$100), filter rotation mechanism (~$150), updated image processing

Accuracy

70-85% standalone, 90-94% fused with vision

Cost Delta

+80% vs baseline

Risk: High cost for marginal accuracy gain; slower dual-capture; complex calibration
