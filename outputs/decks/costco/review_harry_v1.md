# Review: Costco Marketing Deck v1

**Reviewer:** Harry Tannenbaum Persona
**Date:** February 27, 2026
**Overall Score:** 6.5/10
**Verdict:** Revise - Address feedback and resubmit

---

## What Works

- **Supply chain optimization angle is front and center**: Slide 8 ("Waste Data to Procurement Intelligence") is exactly right. This is what differentiates Mill from every waste management company. We're not hauling trash - we're providing intelligence. Good.

- **Costco-specific research is solid**: The 890+ warehouses, $254B revenue, 117M rotisserie chickens - these aren't generic stats. Someone did homework. The food court/bakery/deli angle is smart because that's where waste is concentrated and visible.

- **Conservative ROI assumptions**: The 15-25% reduction target is defensible. I've seen too many decks promise 40% and lose credibility instantly. The $500K-$1M waste per warehouse needs sourcing, but the range approach is smart.

- **Amazon/Whole Foods proof point used correctly**: It's competitive pressure without being desperate. "The category leaders are moving" - that's the right framing.

- **Clear CTA with low-commitment entry**: Option A (pilot) before Option B (strategic partnership) is the right sequence. Let the data sell the bigger deal.

- **Quote attribution**: Using "If you can't measure it, you can't improve it" is on-brand.

---

## What Needs Work

### Issue 1: ROI Math Needs Tightening (Slide 11)
The $75-150M ROI claim is aggressive and undersourced. The math shown gets you to maybe $15-25M on a 50-warehouse pilot:
- 50 x $500K-$1M x 15-25% = $3.75M-$12.5M
- Then 2-3x for procurement = $7.5M-$37.5M

The $75-150M figure implies full rollout but the slide context is pilot. This is the kind of thing that makes finance people dismiss us immediately.

### Issue 2: Chicken Feed Angle is Buried (Slide 6)
"Waste becomes chicken feed ingredient" is just a throwaway bullet. This is actually a massive differentiator - we close the loop. If we're talking to Costco, who sells 117M rotisserie chickens per year, the poultry feed angle should be a whole slide. Connect the dots for them.

### Issue 3: No Specific Amazon/Whole Foods Metrics (Slide 9)
"What they're seeing" gives no numbers. If we have real results, show them. If we don't have permission, say "results under NDA, available under mutual NDA discussion." The current framing feels like we're hiding something.

### Issue 4: Fear-Mongering Language (Slide 14)
"Competitive clock is ticking" is sales-speak. Costco's procurement team will roll their eyes. The 2030 deadline point is legitimate. "Late movers pay premium" is fine. But dial back the urgency rhetoric.

### Issue 5: No Behavior Change Evidence
This is core to Mill's philosophy. We don't just measure waste - we change behavior. Where's the evidence? A single stat like "Store managers using Mill dashboards reduced overproduction by X% in the first 60 days" would be powerful.

### Issue 6: Awkward Framing on Slide 3
"High waste visibility opportunity" tells them their strength is a weakness. Flip this: "Costco's food prep operations are a strategic asset - you have the volume and operational control to turn waste data into competitive advantage."

---

## Recommended Changes

1. **Rework Slide 11 ROI math**: Either show the full calculation transparently, or use the pilot-specific number ($7.5M-$37.5M) with a clear path to scale.

2. **Add a slide on the chicken feed loop**: Connect our output (processed food grounds) to Costco's input (poultry feed for their 117M chickens). Circular economy made tangible.

3. **Get permission to share Amazon/Whole Foods metrics, or be explicit**: "Results exceed projections but are under NDA. We're happy to discuss under mutual confidentiality."

4. **Add one slide on behavior change evidence**: "In our deployments, we see [specific metric] improvement in staff behavior within [timeframe]."

5. **Remove or soften "competitive clock is ticking"**: Replace with factual: "Amazon has already deployed waste intelligence across Whole Foods. The operational advantage compounds over time."

6. **Reframe Slide 3**: Position Costco's food prep as strength, not liability.

7. **Source the $500K-$1M waste per warehouse claim**: Add footnote with methodology or industry benchmark.

---

## Final Note

The deck has good bones. The supply chain angle is right, the Costco research is solid, and the pilot-first approach is smart. But the ROI claims need tightening, and we're missing the behavior change story that makes Mill different from every other waste tech company.

**If this deck gets forwarded internally at Costco without the presenter, will it hold up?** Right now, maybe not. Fix the math, add the behavior change evidence, and make the circular economy story explicit. Then we're ready to ship.
