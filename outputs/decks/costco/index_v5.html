How it works

Three steps from waste to insight

1

Staff dispose food as normal

No change to existing workflows. The bin captures an image and weight automatically when items are added.

2

AI identifies and logs each item

Computer vision classifies the food type, estimates cost, and records the timestamp and location.

3

Data flows to your dashboard

Real-time visibility into what's being wasted, when, and how much it's costing you.

Item-level data enables specific action

Example insights the system could surface

Rotisserie timing

If last batches consistently waste, system recommends moving final cook time earlier.

Prep volume calibration

Day-specific prep waste reveals over-production patterns that can be corrected.

Spoilage patterns

Correlating waste timing with delivery schedules optimizes order frequency.

End-of-day optimization

Data shows exactly when to reduce holding inventory without affecting availability.

Seasonal adjustment

Demand patterns by season inform prep quantities throughout the year.

Location benchmarking

Compare waste across locations to identify training opportunities.

The difference: Generic "reduce waste" advice vs. specific "make 12 fewer pizzas on Tuesdays" recommendations.

The circular opportunity: waste becomes feed

A unique angle for Costco's rotisserie business

Mill doesn't just track waste—it processes it. Our consumer product dehydrates food scraps into nutrient-rich material suitable for chicken feed.

For Costco, this creates a potential circular loop: food waste from food courts could eventually feed the chickens that become Costco rotisserie chickens.

Why this matters

Costco sells 100+ million rotisserie chickens annually. A waste-to-feed story aligns with member sustainability expectations.

Circular vision

Food court waste → Mill processing → Chicken feed → Costco chickens
